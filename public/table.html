    How to Play

    Getting Around

    • Left-drag empty table to orbit your view; right-drag to pan up, down, left and right; scroll with the mouse scroll-wheel to zoom.
    • Middle-click an empty spot on the table — or press P — to send a visual ping that everyone can see.

    Picking Things Up

    • Left-click-hold a piece to pick it up. Move it and let go to drop it — let go while still moving, and you throw it.
    • Use the mouse scroll-wheel while holding something to raise and lower it.
    • W / S (or ↑ / ↓) also raise and lower.
    • Double-left-click a piece to inspect it up close, where you can re-color it or drag to turn it. Click away to close.

    Turning a Piece

    • Holding Alt and then moving your mouse left and right while holding anything turns it in 15° steps; hold Shift as well for smooth, unsnapped turning.
    • Pressing the middle-mouse button while holding anything rotates it in 45° increments.
    • Pressing A / D (or ← / →) while holding anything also rotates it.
    • All of the above work on a multi-selection of objects as well.

    Piece Shortcuts

    • With a piece held or under the cursor: Delete removes it, U stands it upright or lays it flat, G toggles snap-to-grid (when the table has a grid).
    • Right-click any piece except a card for its menu — roll, stand or lay flat, snap to grid, inspect, delete, and whatever else that piece can do.

    Cards & Decks

    • Left-click a card on the table to take it into your hand; Right-click a card to flip it over. A card is the one piece with no menu.
    • Left-click a deck to take its top card into your hand; left-click + drag off a deck to draw the top card and carry it out.
    • Right-click a deck for its menu — shuffle, split, draw to hand, and Move, which you can press and keep dragging to carry the deck straight out of the menu. Right-click-hold + drag still moves it too.
    • Double-left-click a deck to peek at the top card, then F to place it face-up, D face-down, H into your hand, or R to put it back.

    Your private hand

    • Your hand sits at the bottom of the screen and only you can see it. Everyone else sees a face-down fan at your seat.
    • Left-drag (or left-click) a card out onto the table and it lands face-down; right-drag (or right-click) and it lands face-up.
    • Hide Hand tucks it away; Show Hand brings it back.

    Working with several pieces

    • Hold Shift + left-click + drag empty table to box select multiple objects; Hold Shift + left-click an object to add or remove it from a multi-selection.
    • Left-click + drag any selected piece in a group, and the whole group comes with it.
    • On a selection: U stand or lay flat, G snap to grid, F flip every card, R roll every die, H take every card to hand, Delete remove them all.
    • [ and ] turn the whole formation in 45° steps. Esc clears the selection.

    Getting Around

    • One finger on empty table orbits your view; two fingers pan; pinch to zoom.
    • Press and hold empty table to ping a spot for everyone.

    Picking Things Up

    • Touch + hold + drag a piece to pick it up and move it. Let go while still moving to throw it.
    • While you hold a piece, Raise and Lower appear at the edge of the screen — hold either to raise or lower it.
    • Double-tap a piece to inspect it up close. Tap away to close.

    Turning a piece — two fingers

    • While holding a piece with one finger, put a second finger down and twist. The piece turns with your fingers, snapping to 15°.
    • Pinch those same two fingers in and out to lower and raise it. Twist and pinch work together, and the piece stays put while you do either.
    • Turn left and Turn right at the edge of the screen nudge a piece or a selection round a little at a time — hold either to keep going.

    The press-and-hold menu

    • Press and hold any piece for a ring of actions around your finger. What appears depends on the piece: Flip and Take to hand for cards, Roll for dice, Draw to hand, Shuffle and Split for decks, Dispense for dispensers.
    • Every piece also offers Inspect, Stand / lay flat, Snap to grid and Delete.
    • Decks and dispensers offer Move — press it and keep dragging, and the whole thing comes with your finger. (A plain drag deals from them instead, which is why moving needs its own item.)

    Cards & decks

    • Tap a card on the table to take it into your hand.
    • Tap a deck to take its top card; drag off a deck to draw the top card and carry it out.
    • Double-tap a deck to peek at the top card, then use the buttons to place it face-up, face-down, into your hand, or back on the deck.

    Your private hand

    • Your hand sits at the bottom of the screen and only you can see it. Everyone else sees a face-down fan at your seat.
    • Drag a card out with one finger and it lands face-down. Drag a card out with two fingers and it lands face-up — you can add or lift that finger mid-drag to change your mind.
    • Hide hand tucks it away; Show hand brings it back.

    Working with several pieces

    • Turn on the Select tool, then drag empty table to box a selection, or tap pieces to add and remove them.
    • Drag any selected piece and the whole group comes with it.
    • A row of buttons appears for the selection: stand or lay flat, snap to grid, flip, roll, take to hand, and delete.

    Your Seat Button

    • My Seat snaps the camera back to your own seat.
    • Bird's Eye View looks straight down at the table.
    • Lean In zooms in slightly, as if leaning over the table; Lean Out returns.

    Table Actions

    • Dice Box. Shows you your personal dice box, where you can spawn and roll dice. You can also customize the colors of your dice and save them as your default.
    • Library. A quick way to access the library so you can spawn objects.
    • Multi-Select. Enable multi-select mode.

    Other Tools

    • Private Notes. A scratchpad only you can see.
    • Chat.The room's public chat. The icon marks itself when there are messages you have not read.
    • Scoreboard. Shows everyone's scores. (GM-controlled)
    • Timer. A shared stopwatch and countdown.
    • Music. Use this to listen to built-in music.
    • Measure. Access measurement tools.
    • Whiteboard. When a GM has it out, double-click (or double-tap) the board to take control and draw. Pen, eraser and clear appear while you have it; Esc or Done hands it back. While someone else is drawing, the board says who — wait for them to finish.
    • > Settings. Go here to change UI and Volume settings.

    Helper abilities

    • Library. Use this to add dice, decks and objects — built-in or custom — to the table.
    • Scoreboard. Helpers can add, rename, adjust and clear score rows.

    GM abilities

    • Library. Use this to add dice, decks, objects. GM status gives you access to boards, skyboxes, scenes and pre-built games.
    • Scoreboard. GMs can add, rename, adjust and clear score rows.
    • Room info > Players. The list of players can be re-organized manually to suit whatever turn order you desire.
    • Room info > Room Notes. Only GMs can modify this text field, but everyone can see them. Put info like 'house rules' etc here.
    • Room info > Members. Click the buttons here to admit, promote/demote, or kick players.

    GM Room Controls

    • Scenes. View and load custom Scenes.
    • Save Table. Click this to save the table in its current state.
    • Table Settings This opens a menu where you can change table dimensions, set scale options, enable and modify grids. This is also where you can show and position the Whiteboard
    • Reset clears the table of everything, including everyone's private hands.

    On the way

    • An interactive tutorial — learn the table by using it, instead of reading this panel.
    • Table shapes — round, oval and hex tables, not just a resizable rectangle.
    • Hex grids and pieces that occupy more than one cell, for maps and wargames.
    • Fog of war — hide parts of the board until the GM reveals them.
    • Spectator mode — watch a game without taking a seat.

    More to make your own

    • Custom games — save a whole setup as a starter anyone can spawn.
    • Custom dice — your own faces, colours and textures.
    • Custom dispensers — build your own, the way you build decks today.
    • More from a selection — gather loose cards into a deck, stack tiles, and fill a dispenser from what is already on the table.

    Something missing?

    • Open Tabletop is built in the open. If something here is wrong, awkward, or absent, say so — that is how most of this list got written.
